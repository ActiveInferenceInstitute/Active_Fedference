Hierarchical information-gating diagnostic

Study 7 shows that the 3-level agent runs and federates end to end. This supplementary diagnostic asks a narrower question: under a configured surprise threshold,

Hierarchical… (part 2)

does one leaf observation classify the top meta-context as informative or non-gating? The output is a thresholded information-gating diagnostic,

Hierarchical… (part 3)

not a Bayesian-model-evidence comparison or a data-driven proof of the hierarchy’s correct depth.

Hierarchical… (part 4)

The implementation evaluates levels through hierarchical_reduce in the bayesian_model_reduction module of fedference. For each non-leaf level we measure its Bayesian surprise \(\mathrm{KL}(q_i \,\|\, \tilde p_i)\) —

Hierarchical… (part 5)

how far the leaf observation moves that level’s belief \(q_i\) from its top-down prior \(\tilde p_i\).

Hierarchical… (part 6)

The routine labels a level below the configured threshold as prunable and a level above it as kept. Because this is an inference-derived divergence rather than a model re-fit, the labels are conditional on the specified worlds, observation, and threshold.

Hierarchical… (part 7)

The control is directional by construction. We build two 3-level worlds that differ only in the top level’s conditioned priors:

Hierarchical… (part 8)

a degenerate world whose meta-context is non-gating (both meta-context states predict the same context distribution) and an informative world whose meta-context sharply distinguishes the two contexts.

Hierarchical… (part 9)

In the degenerate world, the top level has Bayesian surprise 0.000 nats and is flagged prunable (recovers the two-level structure: Yes). In the informative world, it has 0.328 nats and is kept (Yes).

Hierarchical… (part 10)

Figure 32 shows both configured worlds side by side. Their shared lower-level parameters isolate the top-level gating contrast, but do not establish a generally correct depth-selection procedure.
