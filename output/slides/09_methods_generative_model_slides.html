State space: one shared latent factor

The world holds one hidden factor: the creature’s location on a square grid of side \(L\), giving \(n_s = L^2\) location states. Our sentinel world uses the \(3\times 3\) cardinality illustrated in Friston et al. (2024), Fig. 1, so \(n_s = 9\) — the cardinality pomdp.N_LOCATIONS exposes and experiment_config carries as n_locations.

State space: one shared… (part 2)

This single location factor is precisely the latent the colony gossips about: it is the shared argument of the log-linear pool (9) and of every belief-sharing round (14). Fixing one hidden factor keeps the recovery limits of Section 6 closed-form and exactly testable,

State space: one shared… (part 3)

rather than approximated.

Four categorical tensors: likelihood, transitions, preferences, priors

The generative model is the tuple \((A, B, C, D_0)\) in the discrete active-inference convention: a categorical probability mass function is a non-negative vector summing to one,

Four categorical tensors (part 2)

and a likelihood matrix is shape \((n_o, n_s)\) whose columns (indexed by hidden state) are categorical.

Four categorical tensors (part 3)

Observation likelihood \(A = P(o\mid s)\). Each sentinel observes the creature’s cell through a noisy sensor. With probability acuity the sensor reports the true cell; the residual mass \(1-\text{acuity}\) spreads uniformly over the other \(n_s-1\) cells. With outcome cardinality \(n_o = n_s\),

Four categorical tensors (part 4)

the single location modality is one \((n_s, n_s)\) matrix:

Four categorical tensors (part 5)

\[ \begin{aligned} A_{o s} &= \text{acuity} && \text{if }o=s,\\ A_{o s} &= \frac{1-\text{acuity}}{n_s-1} && \text{if }o\neq s,\\ \sum_o A_{o s} &= 1. \end{aligned} \]

Four categorical tensors (part 6)

The acuity in Equation (15) tunes how peaked the sensor is: high acuity gives a near-diagonal \(A\) that pins the creature; the belief-sharing study deliberately runs the colony at the low acuity \(\text{acuity} = 0.55\), where no single sentinel can resolve the location alone and the colony must pool evidence to do so.

Four categorical tensors (part 7)

When a seeded generator is supplied, each sentinel’s acuity is jittered by a small non-negative perturbation, so a colony carries slightly heterogeneous likelihoods while every column remains a proper pmf.

Four categorical tensors (part 8)

Transition tensor \(B = P(s'\mid s, u)\). The creature moves on the grid under three control paths — still, left, right — so \(B\) has shape \((n_s, n_s, n_u)\) with \(n_u = 3\). still is the deterministic self-loop; left and right decrement and increment the grid column,

Four categorical tensors (part 9)

saturating at the walls (a wall-adjacent move in the wall’s direction is a self-loop).

Four categorical tensors (part 10)

All three controls act on the column index alone, so the creature’s row is preserved and its motion is confined to the horizontal axis of the grid — a deliberate one-dimensional control over the two-dimensional location factor. Every slice \(B_{\cdot\,\cdot\,u}\) is column-normalized by construction,

Four categorical tensors (part 11)

so the transition is a valid categorical for each action.

Four categorical tensors (part 12)

Log-preference \(C\). The sentinel prefers to see the creature near the den (the center cell), encoded as a log-preference vector of shape \((n_o, 1)\) with a positive bump on the center outcome and zero elsewhere.

Four categorical tensors (part 13)

The preferred-outcome distribution that the expected-free-energy decomposition of Section 5.15 uses is \(p_C(o) = \mathrm{softmax}(C)[o]\).

Four categorical tensors (part 14)

Initial prior \(D_0\). The creature is believed to start at the grid center, so \(D_0\) of shape \((n_s, 1)\) places unit mass on the center cell. \(D_0\) enters state inference as the log-prior of the one-step variational update (Equation (16) below).

Four categorical tensors (part 15)

The columns-are-pmfs invariant is not assumed — it is pinned by ISC-15, which checks that every column of \(A\) and of each \(B_{\cdot\,\cdot\,u}\) sums to one.

One-step variational state inference in the grid world

Given an observation \(o\), a sentinel forms a posterior over the creature’s location by a single softmax step (Friston et al. (2024), Eq. 4): the log-prior plus the additive log-likelihood message,

One-step variational state… (part 2)

summed over any conditionally independent modalities \(m\),

One-step variational state… (part 3)

\[ q(s) = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left( \begin{aligned} &\ln D_0(s)\\ &\quad + \textstyle\sum_m \ln A_m[o_m, s] \end{aligned} \right). \]

One-step variational state… (part 4)

The message \(\ln A_m[o_m, \cdot]\) is the row of \(A_m\) that the observed outcome selects; summing messages over modalities makes each modality an additive evidence term — the categorical product-of-experts. The companion variational free energy, the scalar Equation (16) minimizes, is

One-step variational state… (part 5)

\[ \begin{aligned} F[q] &= \mathbb{E}_q\!\left[ \begin{aligned} &\ln q(s) - \ln D_0(s)\\ &\quad - \textstyle\sum_m \ln A_m[o_m, s] \end{aligned} \right]\\ &= \mathrm{KL}\big(q \,\|\, D_0\big) - \mathbb{E}_q\!\big[\textstyle\sum_m \ln A_m[o_m, s]\big]. \end{aligned} \]

One-step variational state… (part 6)

reported in nats. The one-step posterior of Equation (16) is its unique minimizer, where \(F\) equals the negative log model evidence.

One-step variational state… (part 7)

Both live in belief_updating.infer_states and belief_updating.vfe, and the free energy of Equation (17) is the quantity the communicating-versus-incommunicado colony comparison of Section 5.22 scores.

One-step variational state… (part 8)

This inference step is not a separate mechanism bolted onto the colony: it is the \(L=\mathrm{NLL}\), learning-rate-1 special case of the generalized-Bayes posterior Equation (2), reusing the same locked softmax.

One-step variational state… (part 9)

That client identity recovers the stated categorical Bayes substrate at its trusting limits. The separate server identity in Section 5.8 then yields the project log-linear pool under its qualified Eq. 7 message-combination bridge;
