Generalized Bayes (part 7)

Definition (Generalized-(Gibbs)-Bayes posterior)

Given loss \(L\), prior \(\pi_0\), learning rate \(\tau>0\), and divergence \(\mathcal D\), the generalized-Bayes posterior \(q_n^\ast\) minimizes (2). When \(\mathcal D=\mathrm{KL}\), its exact categorical minimizer is the tempered softmax in (3).

Generalized Bayes (part 8)

\[ q_n^\ast(s) \;\propto\; \pi_0(s)\,\exp\!\big(-\tau \textstyle\sum_i L(s; o_i)\big), \]

Generalized Bayes (part 9)

The implementation surface is the generalized_posterior function in the generalized_bayes module.

Generalized Bayes (part 10)

The tempered softmax of Equation (3), stated in the definition above, is not an approximation: it is the exact closed-form minimizer of Equation (2) when the regularizer is the KL divergence, because the categorical support is finite and the objective is strictly convex in \(q\).

Generalized Bayes (part 15)

FedGVI computes each client update against a cavity rather than the full posterior, so a contributing agent does not double-count its own previous message. We name that operation.

Generalized Bayes (part 16)

Definition (Cavity / PVI factor update)

The cavity is the normalized removal of agent \(n\)'s site factor from the colony posterior in natural-parameter space, as in (4). PVI replaces the site factor and recombines it with that cavity according to (5).

Generalized Bayes (part 17)

\[ \begin{aligned} q_{-n}(s) &= \frac{q(s)/t_n(s)}{\sum_{s'} q(s')/t_n(s')} \\ &= \operatorname{softmax}\!\big(\log q(s)-\log t_n(s)\big), \end{aligned} \]

Conjugate likelihood learning for the shared model

Active-inference agents learn the parameters of their generative model, not just plan with them (Smith et al. 2022; Friston et al. 2024).

Conjugate likelihood… (part 2)

The likelihood matrix \(A\) carries a Dirichlet prior with concentration \(a\) over each column, updated conjugately by accumulating observation-state co-occurrence counts (18),

Conjugate likelihood… (part 3)

giving the column-normalized expected likelihood. The update of Equation (18) is driven by the expected sufficient statistics under the data-generating model,

so as the concentrations accumulate \(\mathbb{E}[A]\) converges to the true likelihood.

Conjugate likelihood… (part 4)

Convergence is measured by the per-column KL divergence summed over hidden states, which decreases monotonically toward the standard-Bayes fixed point; Section 7.3 reports the learning curve, where the KL falls from 3.4231 to 0.0027 across 24 count batches.

Conjugate likelihood… (part 5)

A forgetting hyperprior optionally decays the running mass toward an asymptote so the agent does not become infinitely confident; with the hyperprior disabled the classical unbounded accumulation of Equation (18) is recovered.

Conjugate likelihood… (part 6)

The implementation is learn_likelihood in the dirichlet_learning module.

Bayesian model reduction for structure comparison

Structure learning in the active-inference frame proceeds by Bayesian model reduction (BMR): given a full model with Dirichlet posterior post under prior prior,

Bayesian model reduction… (part 2)

the change in negative variational free energy from swapping in a reduced prior — for example one that prunes a redundant column toward zero —

Bayesian model reduction… (part 3)

is available in closed form without re-running inference (Friston and Penny 2011; Smith et al. 2022).

Bayesian model reduction… (part 4)

Because the likelihood is shared, the reduced posterior is post + reduced_prior - prior, and the free-energy difference is a difference of log multivariate Beta functions (20), where \(\ln B(a) = \sum_k \ln\Gamma(a_k) - \ln\Gamma(\sum_k a_k)\) is the log Dirichlet normalizer.

Bayesian model reduction… (part 5)

A positive \(\Delta F\) in Equation (20) means the reduced model has more evidence — the pruned structure was redundant and should be adopted; a negative \(\Delta F\) means the reduction destroyed something the data support. When the reduced prior equals the prior the score is identically zero,

Bayesian model reduction… (part 6)

the no-reduction fixed point.

Section 7.4 reports \(\Delta F = 3.68\) for a redundant reduction (accepted) against \(\Delta F = -27.67\) for a supported one (rejected). The implementation is bayesian_model_reduction.reduce.
