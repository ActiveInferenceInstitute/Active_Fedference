Limitations and claim boundaries — presentation

Limitations and claim boundaries

The boundaries here define the contribution. The central goal is to show — concretely and testably — that the categorical generalized-variational construction inspired by FedGVI (Mildner et al. 2025) has standard-Bayes client limits and a project-local log-linear-pool server identity.

Scope boundaries that the… (part 18)

Discrete POMDP only—continuous or hybrid state spaces are not addressed. This work is discrete-categorical only, matching the community’s worked POMDP example (Da Costa et al. 2020; Friston et al. 2024).

Scope boundaries that the… (part 19)

The limit-as-proof contract is validated off the categorical case only for a one-dimensional Gaussian-mean conjugate slice; continuous-state active inference and Gaussian belief-sharing colonies remain untested.

Scope boundaries that the… (part 20)

Temperature and divergence calibration are fixed, not learned. Coarsened posterior and safe-Bayes theory make clear that the learning-rate/temperature is part of the inference rule under misspecification (Miller and Dunson 2018; Grünwald 2012; Kleijn and Vaart 2012).

Scope boundaries that the… (part 21)

This manuscript validates the configured losses and divergences, but it does not learn an optimal coarsening radius, temperature, or divergence schedule across agents.

Scope boundaries that the… (part 22)

Real multi-machine federation, networking, and privacy cryptography. Federation here is mathematical (factor aggregation), not infrastructural— unlike communication-efficient or Byzantine-robust federated learning (McMahan et al. 2017; Blanchard et al. 2017).

Scope boundaries that the… (part 23)

New linguistic theory. The language-acquisition study (Section 7.3) reproduces the Dirichlet count mechanism (18) mechanically; it does not extend the linguistics.

What the statistics can and cannot claim

The verdict is a paired comparison at a single high contamination rate (Section 7.5.2, Table 7). The design concentrates power where the effect is largest.

What the statistics can and… (part 2)

It therefore identifies where robustness pays off, but it does not characterize contamination as a continuous function.

What the statistics can and… (part 3)

The per-rate effect and inference projections (Table 5; Table 6) describe the remaining sweep.

What the statistics can and… (part 4)

Their inferential claims do not exceed the declared BH-FDR control (Benjamini and Hochberg 1995).

What the statistics can and… (part 5)

BH-FDR controls expected false discovery proportion within the declared family, not the chance of any false positive. The matched-pairs Wilcoxon tests are rank tests under paired-difference assumptions (Wilcoxon 1945; Fay and Proschan 2010), not assumption-free proofs about raw means.

What the statistics can and… (part 6)

Confidence intervals are percentile bootstrap (Efron and Tibshirani 1993), not analytic, and inherit that method’s small-sample caveats at the lowest trial counts.

What the statistics can and… (part 7)

The power values are observed-effect design approximations useful for confirmatory sample-size planning; they are not independent evidence for the verdict and do not cover model-specification uncertainty outside the seeded simulation harness (Wasserstein and Lazar 2016).
